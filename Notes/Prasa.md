**Definition** Prasa (ప్రాస) is the prosodic rhyming rule in Telugu poetry governing the second syllable of each line (*pada*) in a stanza.

**Meter Eligibility**

* **Required:** Standard *Vrittas* (Utpalamala, Champakamala, Shardulamu, Mattebhamu), select *Jatis* (Kandamu, Utsahamu, Dwipada), and various special meters (Mattakokila, Sragdhara, Malini, etc.).  
* **Excluded:** *Upajatis* (Seesamu, Tetagiti, Ataveladi, Manjari Dwipada) and *Dandakamu* (which lacks both Prasa and Yati rules).  
* **Determinant:** A meter's specific rule definition (*lakshanam*) solely states **whether** Prasa is present or absent.

**Universal Mechanics**

* **Uniformity:** Prasa rules operate identically across all meters that enforce them.  
* **Consistency:**  
* Permissible rhyming pairs (e.g., **'స'** and **'శ'**) are valid in every meter that uses Prasa.  
* Impermissible combinations (e.g., **'డ'** and **'ఢ'**) are universally invalid across all meters.  
* **Independence:** Individual meter types do not have custom Prasa variations; once Prasa is designated as present, the complete, standard body of Prasa rules applies in full.

&nbsp;

&nbsp;

&nbsp;

* **Consonant-only constraint:** Prasa rules apply strictly to consonants (*hallulu* / హల్లులు), not to vowels (*achulu* / అచ్చులు).  
* **Consonant identity:** The consonant occupying the Prasa position (the second syllable of each line) must be identical across all lines of the stanza.  
* **Vowel neutrality:** There are no rules or restrictions governing the vowels combined with the Prasa consonants; the vowels across lines may be identical or completely different without violating the meter.  
* **Structural separation:** In practice, Telugu consonants never appear in isolation in poetry—they always combine with a vowel, but only the base consonant determines the Prasa match (e.g., in syllables **లు**, **ల**, **లి**, **ల**, the shared consonant is **'ల్'** (*l*), while the vowels **ఉ**, **అ**, **ఇ**, **అ** vary freely).

&nbsp;

&nbsp;

* **Rule reinforced:** Prasa is established solely by the identity of the base consonant (*hallu* / హల్లు); variations in the attached vowels (*achulu* / అచ్చులు) across lines do not affect the validity of the rhyme.  
* **Example 3 (Shardulamu – Mahabharata, Adi Parva 1-111):**  
* **Syllables:** తా, తో, తి, త  
* **Consonant:** Identical consonant **'త్'** (*ta-karamu*) across all lines, forming **'త'కారప్రాస** (*ta-kara prasa*).  
* **Vowels:** Entirely different across all four lines: **ఆ**, **ఓ**, **ఇ**, **అ** (త్+ఆ, త్+ఓ, త్+ఇ, త్+అ).  
* **Example 4 (Mattebhamu – Mahabharata, Adi Parva 1-12):**  
* **Syllables:** వి, వు, వ, వ  
* **Consonant:** Identical consonant **'వ్'** (*va-karamu*) across all lines, forming **'వ'కారప్రాస** (*va-kara prasa*).  
* **Vowels:** Varying in lines 1, 2, and 3 (**ఇ**, **ఉ**, **అ**), and identical in lines 3 and 4 (**అ**).  
* **Example 5 (Kandamu – Bhagavata Purana 1-137):**  
* **Syllables:** రు, రా, ర, రా  
* **Consonant:** Identical consonant **'ర్'** (*rephamu* / రేఫము) across all lines, forming **రేఫప్రాస** (*repha prasa*).  
* **Vowels:** Varying across lines 1, 2, and 3 (**ఉ**, **ఆ**, **అ**), and identical in lines 2 and 4 (**ఆ**).  
* **Identical vowels permissible:** While the rule only mandates consonant uniformity, the attached vowels across all lines may also be entirely identical by coincidence or design without impacting the Prasa requirement.  
* **Consonant-governed validity:** Regardless of whether the vowels vary (as shown in earlier examples) or match uniformly across every line, the Prasa is named and governed strictly by its base consonant (*hallu* / హల్లు).

&nbsp;

**Examples of Identical Vowel Occurrences**

* **Example 6 (*Utpalamala* – Bhagavata Purana 1-3):**  
* **Syllables:** త, త, త, త  
* **Consonant:** **'త్'** (*ta-karamu*), forming **'త'కారప్రాస**.  
* **Vowels:** Consistently **అ** across all four lines ($త \= త్ \+ అ$).  
* **Example 7 (*Utpalamala* – Bhagavata Purana 1-6):**  
* **Syllables:** ణి, ణి, ణి, ణి  
* **Consonant:** **'ణ్'** (*na-karamu*), forming **'ణ'కారప్రాస**.  
* **Vowels:** Consistently **ఇ** across all four lines ($ణి \= ణ్ \+ ఇ$).  
* **Example 8 (*Utpalamala* – Bhagavata Purana 1-209):**  
* **Syllables:** యు, యు, యు, యు  
* **Consonant:** **'య్'** (*ya-karamu*), forming **'య'కారప్రాస**.  
* **Vowels:** Consistently **ఉ** across all four lines ($యు \= య్ \+ ఉ$).

**Analytical Takeaway**

* These cases provide empirical evidence that Prasa restricts only consonants, not vowels; whether vowels differ completely or remain identical across lines, the metrical validity of the Prasa is unaffected.

**Prasākṣaramu – R̥tva Sahitamu (Vocalic 'R̥' / Vṭruvasuḍi in Prasa)**

* **Vowel status of 'ఋ' and 'ౠ':** The vocalic signs *R̥-tvamu* (వట్రువసుడి / ృ) and *R̥̄-tvamu* (ౄ) are grammatically vowels (*achulu* / అచ్చులు).  
* **No uniformity requirement for *R̥-tvamu*:** If a consonant in the Prasa position takes a *vatruvasudi* (e.g., **కృ**, **ఘృ**, **జృ**, **తృ**, **తౄ**, **పృ**), the remaining lines do **not** need to carry *vatruvasudi*. Just like any other vowel, its presence is unconstrained across lines.  
* **Equivalence across vowel combinations:** Just as **'క'** and **'కి'** form a valid Prasa pair through their shared consonant **'క్'**, **'క'** and **'కృ'** are equally valid.  
* **Distinction between *R̥-tvamu* and Conjunct Consonants (*Samyuktākṣaramulu*):**  
* In pronunciation, a consonant combined with *R̥-tvamu* sounds similar to a consonant conjunct with *repha* (**'ర్'**), but phonologically they are distinct.  
* **కృ** $= \\text{క్} \+ \\text{ఋ}$ (single consonant \+ vowel $\\rightarrow$ **not** a conjunct / *asamyuktam*).  
* **క్రు** $= \\text{క్} \+ \\text{ర్} \+ \\text{ఉ}$ (two consonants \+ vowel $\\rightarrow$ **conjunct** / *samyuktākṣaram*).  
* Because their underlying phonemes (*varnas*) differ, *R̥-tvamu* is clarified explicitly so it is not mistakenly scanned or treated as a consonant cluster.  
* **Theoretical vs. Practical Usage of *R̥̄-tvamu*:** While syllables with long *R̥̄-tvamu* like **'తౄ'** (in words like *mātṟ̄ṇamu*, *pitṟ̄ṇamu*) theoretically validate Prasa against simple **'త'**\-consonants (**త**, **తి**, **తు**, **తా**), historical poetic usage (*kāvya prayōgamulu*) of long *R̥̄* in Prasa is virtually absent. Practical examples are primarily confined to short *R̥* (**ఋ**).

&nbsp;

&nbsp;

**Prasākṣaramu – ఌ-kāra Sahitamu (Vocalic 'L̥' / ఌ-kāramu in Prasa)**

* **Vowel status of 'ఌ' and 'ౡ':** The vocalic signs *ఌ* and *ౡ* are categorized strictly as vowels (*achulu* / అచ్చులు).  
* **Application of vowel rule:** Because Prasa rules enforce uniformity solely on consonants and ignore vowels, a consonant bearing *ఌ-kāramu* validly pairs with the identical consonant carrying any other vowel in the remaining lines.  
* **Lexical rarity:** Words featuring *ఌ-kāramu* are virtually non-existent in usage, with the rare exception of **'కౢప్తి'** (*kḷpti* \= arrangement/creation) and **'కౢప్తము'** (*kḷptamu* \= arranged/created). In modern usage, this is commonly replaced by the conjunct form **'క్లుప్త'** (*klu-pta* \= abridged/condensed, where *k* and *l* form a conjunct consonant).  
* **Attested poetic usage:** Classical poetic occurrences of *ఌ-kāramu* in Prasa are extremely rare, with only a single notable instance recorded.

**Example Analysis (*Shardulamu* – Rāghava Pāṇḍavīyamu 1-3)**

* **Prasa syllables:** **క**, **కౢ**, **క**, **కి**  
* **Consonant breakdown:**  
* Line 1: **క** ($\\text{క్} \+ \\text{అ}$)  
* Line 2: **కౢ** ($\\text{క్} \+ \\text{ఌ}$)  
* Line 3: **క** ($\\text{క్} \+ \\text{అ}$)  
* Line 4: **కి** ($\\text{క్} \+ \\text{ఇ}$)  
* **Validity:** All four lines share the identical consonant **'క్'** (*ka-karamu*), validly fulfilling **'క'కారప్రాస** (*ka-kara prasa*) despite Line 2 carrying the vocalic vowel *ఌ*.  
* **Clarification:** This is not a distinct or special variety of Prasa, but simply a reaffirmation of the fundamental principle that vowel variations—even rare vocalic vowels like *ఌ*—do not impact Prasa constraints.

**Prasākṣaramu – Bindu Sahitamu (Anusvara with the Prasa Syllable)**

* **Types of Bindu (Anusvara):**  
* **Pūrṇabindu:** Also called *Pūrṇānusvāramu* or *Niṇḍusunna* (full circle / నిండుసున్న / ం). Present in both Sanskrit and Telugu.  
* **Ardhabindu:** Also called *Ardhānusvāramu*, *Arasunna* (half-circle / అరసున్న / ఁ), or *Khaṇḍabindu*. Present in Telugu, but absent in classical Sanskrit.  
* **Phonetic Dependence on Vowels:**  
* The bindu is an *anusvāra* that strictly follows a vowel (*svara* / *acchu*), never a pure consonant alone.  
* Syllable construction follows: Consonant \+ Vowel \+ Bindu (e.g., **కం** \= $\\text{క్} \+ \\text{అ} \+ \\text{ం}$; **కిం** \= $\\text{క్} \+ \\text{ఇ} \+ \\text{ం}$). A standalone consonant cannot directly take a bindu (e.g., $\\text{క్ం}$ does not form an independent syllable in classical Sanskrit or Telugu).  
* **No Uniformity Requirement for Bindu:**  
* "Bindu sahitamu" denotes that a Prasa syllable is followed by an anusvara (e.g., **కం**, **కిం**, **గం**, **గాం**).  
* If one line has a bindu-bearing Prasa syllable, the corresponding syllables in the remaining lines **do not need** to have a bindu.  
* Mixed combinations across lines are entirely valid, such as **కం**, **క**, **కి**, **కు** sharing the base consonant **'క్'**.  
* **Rule Foundation:**  
* Because Prasa constraints apply solely to consonants and do not restrict vowels, they likewise **do not restrict anusvaras**, which merely follow and attach to those vowels.

&nbsp;

**Prasākṣaramu – Bindupūrvakamu (Bindu Preceding the Prasa Syllable)**

Unlike a bindu that *follows* the Prasa syllable (which is unrestricted), a bindu positioned **before** (*pūrvamuna*) the Prasa syllable directly impacts the Prasa rule.

* **Pūrṇabindu Preceding Prasa (Strict Identity Rule):**  
* If the Prasa syllable in Line 1 is preceded by a full bindu (*pūrṇabindu* / నిండుసున్న), then the Prasa syllables in **all subsequent lines must also be preceded by a full bindu**.  
* **Example:** If Line 1 starts with a word like **'నిండు...'** (*niṇḍu...*), the Prasa syllable **'డు'** is preceded by a full bindu (making it a *pūrṇabindupūrvaka ‘da’-kāramu*). Therefore, lines 2, 3, and 4 must each have a *‘da’-kāramu* preceded by a full bindu.  
* This forms a specific prosodic variation known as **Pūrṇabindu Prāsamu** (పూర్ణబిందుప్రాసము).  
* **Ardhabindu Preceding Prasa (Flexible Rule):**  
* If the Prasa syllable in Line 1 is preceded by a half bindu (*ardhabindu* / *arasunna* / ఁ), there is **no strict requirement** for all subsequent lines to have an ardhabindu before their Prasa syllables.  
* **Option A – Uniform Ardhabindu:** If all lines consistently feature an ardhabindu before the Prasa consonant (e.g., matching a starting pattern like **'వాఁడి...'** across all lines), it is classified as **Ardhabindu Prāsamu** (అర్ధబిందుప్రాసము).  
* **Option B – Mixed Occurrence:** If some lines have an ardhabindu preceding the Prasa syllable while others do not (i.e., pure vowels without a half-circle), the rhyme remains completely valid. This mixed pattern is termed **Khaṇḍākhaṇḍa Prāsamu** (ఖండాఖండప్రాసము).

&nbsp;

&nbsp;

**Prasākṣaramu – Visarga (Visarga Associated with the Prasa Syllable)**

* **Visarga Following the Prasa Syllable (*Visargasahitamu*):**  
* If a Prasa syllable carries a visarga after it (e.g., **కః**, **తః**, **యః**), there is **no requirement** for the Prasa syllables in the other lines to also carry a visarga.  
* Mixed combinations across lines are entirely valid as long as the base consonant matches.  
* **Example (*Shardulamu* – Mahabharata, Adi Parva 3-11):**  
* **Prasa syllables:** **యు**, **యః**, **యై**, **యు**  
* Line 2 has a visarga-bearing syllable (**యః**), while lines 1, 3, and 4 do not.  
* Because the base consonant across all lines remains **'య్'** (*ya-karamu*), it validly fulfills **'య'కారప్రాస** (*ya-kara prasa*).  
* **Visarga Preceding the Prasa Syllable (*Visargapūrvakamu*):**  
* If the Prasa syllable is preceded by a visarga, that visarga **must be observed strictly** across all lines.  
* If Line 1 has a visarga preceding its Prasa consonant, the Prasa consonants in all subsequent lines must also be preceded by a visarga.  
* **Example:** If Line 1 begins with **'దుఃఖ...'** (*duḥkha...*), the Prasa syllable is **'ఖ'**, which is preceded by a visarga (forming a *visargapūrvaka ‘kha’-kāramu*). All other lines must likewise feature a *‘kha’-kāramu* preceded by a visarga.  
* This mandatory alignment constitutes a specific rule variation termed **Visarga Prāsamu** (విసర్గప్రాసము).

&nbsp;

&nbsp;

**Prasākṣaramu – Saṁyuktamu (Conjunct Consonants in the Prasa Position)**

* **Compulsory conjunct matching:** If the Prasa syllable is a conjunct consonant (*saṁyuktākṣaramu* / సంయుక్తాక్షరము), the Prasa syllables in all remaining lines must also be conjunct consonants.  
* **Strict order and identity of consonants:** The exact consonants and their sequence (*kramamu*) within the cluster must match identically across all lines:  
* The constituent consonants that combine in Line 1 must appear in the exact same order in lines 2, 3, and 4\.  
* Reversing or altering the constituent consonants violates the rhyme.  
* **Examples of consonant order dependency:**  
* If Line 1 starts with **'తర్క...'** (*tarka...*), the Prasa syllable is **'ర్క'** (a combination of *repha* \+ *ka-kāra* / **ర్ \+ క**). Therefore, the Prasa syllables in all subsequent lines must strictly be **ర్క** (*repha-ka conjuncts*).  
* If Line 1 starts with **'చక్రి...'** (*cakri...*), the Prasa syllable is **'క్రి'** (a combination of *ka-kāra* \+ *repha* / **క్ \+ ర**). Therefore, the Prasa syllables in all subsequent lines must strictly be **క్ర**\-based (*ka-repha conjuncts*).  
* **Classification:** This strict requirement forms a specialized prosodic category designated as **Saṁyuktākṣara Prāsamu** (సంయుక్తాక్షరప్రాసము).

&nbsp;

&nbsp;

**Prasākṣaramu – Sandhigatamu (Prasa Resulting from Sandhi)**

* **Governed by the Post-Sandhi Consonant:**  
* *Sandhigatamu* refers to a syllable formed via *sandhi* (phonetic compounding).  
* Since Prasa constraints apply solely to consonants and never to vowels, when a Prasa syllable forms through vowel *sandhi* (*svara sandhi*), the Prasa match is evaluated strictly using the resulting base consonant.  
* **Contrast with Yati Mechanics:**  
* In *Yati*, vowels are strictly regulated; under *svara sandhi*, Yati often targets the initial vowel of the second component word (*para-padādi svara* / *svara pradhāna yati*).  
* In Prasa, pre-sandhi forms are completely irrelevant; Prasa is determined solely by the final phonetic shape (*śabda svarūpamu*) after *sandhi* has taken place.  
* **Valid Matching of Inherent and Sandhi-Derived Forms:**  
* Syllables formed naturally in the base word (*śabda-siddhamu*) and syllables created dynamically through *sandhi* (*sandhigatamu*) can be freely matched with one another.  
* This rule holds true for syllables resulting from consonant *sandhi* (*hallu sandhi*) as well as vowel *sandhi*.

**Example Analysis (*Kandamu* – Appakavīyamu 3-297)**

* **Prasa syllables:** **న**, **నూ**, **న**, **ని**  
* **Origin of syllables:**  
* Lines 1 & 3: Inherently formed words (*śabda-siddhamu*).  
* Line 2: Formed via sandhi: తాను \+ ఊరక \= **తానూరక** (syllable **నూ**).  
* Line 4: Formed via sandhi: క్రియచేన్ \+ ఇఁక(న్) \= **క్రియచేనిఁక(న్)** (syllable **ని**).  
* **Validity:** Because all four lines share the identical consonant **'న్'** (*na-karamu*), the stanza validly satisfies **'న'కారప్రాస** (*na-kara prasa*).  
* **Classification:** This occurrence is formally categorized as **Sandhigata Prāsamu** (సంధిగతప్రాసము).

**Prasākṣaramu – Drutamu (Drutamu / 'న్' Associated with Prasa)**

* **Drutamu Following the Prasa Syllable:**  
* When a *drutamu* (**న్**) immediately follows a Prasa syllable, it carries **no restriction** in Prasa matching.  
* Although the attached *drutamu* makes the preceding Prasa syllable prosodically long (*guru*), the Prasa match is evaluated solely on the primary consonant of that syllable.  
* In classical manuscript traditions, the *drutamu* typically merges (*saṁśleṣamu*) with the subsequent consonant (e.g., written as **ప్రీతిన్ వైరి...** $\\rightarrow$ **ప్రీతిన్వైరి...**). The Prasa syllable remains purely **తి** (consonant **'త్'**).  
* **Example (*Shardulamu* – Mahabharata, Adi Parva 1-78):**  
* **Prasa syllables:** **తా**, **తో**, **తి**, **త** (Line 3 has **తిన్**, but scans as **తి**).  
* Fulfills valid **'త'కారప్రాస** (*ta-kara prasa*) regardless of the trailing *drutamu*.  
* **Drutamu Preceding the Prasa Syllable:**  
* A *drutamu* positioned **before** (*mundu*) the Prasa syllable **must be strictly observed** across all lines.  
* A preceding *drutamu* cannot be arbitrarily split or isolated to claim a simple consonant match; it either forms a conjunct consonant (*saṁśleṣamu*) or transforms into a *bindu* via *sandhi*.  
* **Invalid Example (Syllable Splitting):**  
* Attempting to pair lines ending/starting with: **దేవర... / నీవు... / కావున... / నిన్ వదలి...**  
* In Line 4, **నిన్ \+ వదలి** coalesces into **నిన్వదలి**. The actual Prasa syllable becomes the conjunct **'న్వ'** (*nva* \= *na-kāra* \+ *va-kāra*), breaking Prasa (*prāsa-bhaṅgamu*) against the pure **'వ'** consonants of lines 1–3.  
* **Valid Conjunct Example:**  
* If all four lines share the identical conjunct: **అన్వయ... / మన్వంతర... / ధన్వంతరి... / నిన్వదలి...**  
* This validly forms **'న్వ'కార సంయుక్తాక్షర ప్రాస** (*na-kāra, va-kāra conjunct prasa*).  
* **Sandhi Transformation to Bindu:**  
* When a *drutamu* precedes a hard consonant (*paruṣamu*), the consonant softens to a *saralamu* and the *drutamu* becomes a *bindu* (e.g., **పుత్రున్ కని...** $\\rightarrow$ **పుత్రుంగని...**).  
* The Prasa syllable becomes a *bindu-pūrvaka ‘ga’-kāramu* (**ంగ**), which must then comply with the rules of **Bindupūrvaka Prāsamu** / **Sandhigata Prāsamu**.

**Prasākṣaramu – Pollu 'La'-kāramu (Pure Consonant 'ల్' Associated with Prasa)**

* **Definition of Pollu 'La'-kāramu:** Refers to the vowelless, pure consonant **'ల్'** (*l*).  
* **Pollu 'ల్' Following the Prasa Syllable:**  
* When a pure consonant **'ల్'** immediately follows a Prasa syllable, it carries **no restriction** in Prasa matching.  
* While the following **'ల్'** turns the Prasa syllable into a prosodically heavy (*guru*) syllable, the Prasa match is evaluated solely on the primary consonant of that syllable.  
* In ancient scribal practice, **'ల్'** is conjunctively written with the succeeding consonant (e.g., written modernly as **రావుల్ దప్పెను...** for ease of reading, but traditionally as **రావుల్దప్పెను...**). The Prasa syllable itself remains **వు** (consonant **'వ్'**).  
* **Example (*Shardulamu* – Bhagavata Purana 8-90):**  
* **Prasa syllables:** **వొ**, **వు**, **వే**, **వే** (Line 2 has **వుల్**, but scans as **వు**).  
* Validly fulfills **'వ'కారప్రాస** (*va-kara prasa*) regardless of the trailing **'ల్'**.  
* **Pollu 'ల్' Preceding the Prasa Syllable:**  
* A pure consonant **'ల్'** appearing **before** (*mundu*) the Prasa syllable **must be strictly observed** across all lines.  
* Just like the rule for *drutamu* (**న్**), an isolated **'ల్'** naturally fuses (*saṁśleṣamu*) with the succeeding consonant to form a conjunct consonant (*saṁyuktākṣaramu*). It cannot be artificially detached to claim a simple consonant rhyme.  
* **Invalid Example (Illegitimate Splitting):**  
* Attempting: **కోవెల... / సేవ... / భావము... / పూవుల్ విరియ...**  
* In Line 4, **పూవుల్ \+ విరియ** fuses into **పూవుల్విరియ**. The actual Prasa syllable becomes the conjunct **'ల్వి'** (*lvi* \= *la-kāra* \+ *va-kāra*), causing a metrical violation (*prāsa-bhaṅgamu*) against the simple **'వ'** consonants of lines 1–3.  
* **Valid Conjunct Example:**  
* If all four lines share the identical cluster: **చెల్వ... / కల్వ... / కొల్వ... / పూవుల్విరియ...**  
* This validly forms **'ల్వ'కార సంయుక్తాక్షర ప్రాస** (*la-kāra, va-kāra conjunct prasa*).

**Prāsa – Dantya 'Ca' and 'Ja' (5.1.12)**

* **Homorganic status (*Savarna*):** By grammatical rule (*Bāla Vyākaraṇam*, Samjñā 7: *"dantya tālavyaṁbulayina cajalu savarṇaṁbulu"*), dental and palatal forms of **'చ'** and **'జ'** are considered homogeneous / phonetically equivalent (*savarṇamulu*).  
* **Valid Prasa pairings:**  
* Dental **'చ'** (ౘ) and Palatal **'చ'** (చ) form a valid Prasa match.  
* Dental **'జ'** (ౙ) and Palatal **'జ'** (జ) form a valid Prasa match.  
* **Classification:** These pairings are considered pure identity rhymes (**Samaprāsamulu** / సమప్రాసలు).

---

**Prāsamaitri – Rhyme Compatibility Between Different Consonants (5.1.13)**

* **General rule (*Samaprāsa*):** Under standard rules, all four lines must feature the identical consonant in the Prasa position.  
* **Prāsa Bhedamulu (Prosodic Variations & Relaxations):**  
* Beyond absolute identity, prosody permits certain distinct consonants to rhyme with each other.  
* When two different consonants are permitted to pair in the Prasa slot, they are said to possess **Prāsamaitri** (ప్రాసమైత్రి / rhyme friendliness/affinity).  
* **Phonetic basis for compatibility:**  
* **Articulatory similarity (*Uccāraṇa Sāmyamu*):** Prāsamaitri is permitted primarily when the consonants share significant phonetic proximity or acoustic affinity in pronunciation.  
* **Permissible pair example:** **'స'** and **'శ'** share articulatory closeness, making them valid partners in Prasa across lines of a stanza.  
* **Basis for incompatibility:**  
* **Lack of phonetic similarity:** When distinct consonants have clear, stark differences in articulation, they lack Prāsamaitri and cannot rhyme together.  
* **Impermissible pair example:** **'డ'** and **'ఢ'** have distinctly different phonetic properties (unaspirated retroflex vs. aspirated retroflex); therefore, pairing them in Prasa is strictly invalid (*prāsa chelladu*).

**Prāsa Pūrvākṣara Niyama Prayōjanamu (Significance & Utility of the Pre-Prasa Syllable Rule)**

* **Automatic Compliance in Regular Vrittas (*Sama Vrittas*):**  
* In *Sama Vrittas*, the rule requires no active effort because the fixed gana structure (*niyamita gaṇa-kramamu*) inherently guarantees uniformity across all four lines.  
* **Utpalamala & Shardulamu:** Every line begins with a gana whose first syllable is a heavy syllable (*guru*—e.g., *Bha-gana* `U | |` in Utpalamala); hence, the pre-Prasa syllables across all four lines naturally become Gurus.  
* **Champakamala & Mattebhamu:** Every line starts with a light syllable (*laghu*); hence, all pre-Prasa syllables naturally become Laghus.  
* **Deliberate Enforcement in Jati Meters (e.g., *Kandamu*):**  
* Because Jati meters like *Kandamu* allow multiple interchangeable ganas (*Bha, Ja, Sa, Nala, Gaga*), the poet must actively select compatible initial ganas to satisfy the rule.  
* **Initial Syllables of Kandamu Ganas:**  
* Start with **Guru (`U`)**: **భ** (`U | |`), **గగ** (`U U`)  
* Start with **Laghu (`|`)**: **జ** (`| U |`), **స** (`| | U`), **నల** (`| | | |`)  
* **The Guru Constraint:** If Line 1 starts with **భ** or **గగ**, the first syllable is a Guru. Therefore, lines 2, 3, and 4 **cannot** start with *జ*, *స*, or *నల*; they must strictly begin with **భ** or **గగ**.  
* **The Laghu Constraint:** If Line 1 starts with **స** or **నల**, the first syllable is a Laghu. Therefore, the subsequent lines **cannot** start with *భ* or *గగ*; they must begin with a Laghu-starting gana (*జ, స, నల* in even lines; *స, నల* in Line 3).  
* Consequently, the rule imposes a functional, structural constraint on gana selection in *Kandamu* and other Jati meters.  
* **Exception in Asymmetrical Vrittas (*Asama Vrittas*):**  
* *Asama Vrittas* (meters where the lines have unequal metrical structures, such as *Upajāti Vṛttamulu*) do **not** enforce the Pre-Prasa Syllable rule.  
* This stands as a recognized prosodic exception (*apavādamu*) to the general uniformity of Prasa rules.

**Sūkṣmāṁśamulu – Nuances of the Pre-Prasa Syllable Rule (5.2.2)**

* **Prosodic Weight Over Vowel Length (*Guru-Laghu vs. Hrasva-Dīrgha*):**  
* The rule strictly regulates metric weight (**Guru / Laghu**), not inherent phonetic length (**Hrasva / Dīrgha**).  
* A short vowel (*hrasva*) that becomes heavy (*guru*) by preceding a conjunct consonant fully satisfies the requirement alongside an inherently long vowel (*dīrgha*).  
* Lines can mix naturally short and naturally long vowels, provided all resulting syllables evaluate to the identical weight (**all Gurus** or **all Laghus**).

**Example 3 (*Kandamu* – Mahabharata, Adi Parva 1-134):**

* **Initial/Pre-Prasa Syllables:** **కు**, **రా**, **ర**, **నా**  
* **కు** (*hrasva* vowel 'ఉ', but *guru* because it precedes the conjunct 'క్షి')  
* **రా** (*dīrgha* vowel 'ఆ', inherently *guru*)  
* **ర** (*hrasva* vowel 'అ', but *guru* because it precedes the conjunct 'క్షిం')  
* **నా** (*dīrgha* vowel 'ఆ', inherently *guru*)  
* All four syllables function prosodically as **Gurus**, perfectly fulfilling the rule despite alternating between short and long vowels.

---

* **Weight Rule Applies Solely to Pre-Prasa Syllables, Not the Prasa Syllables:**  
* The mandate for uniform metric weight (**all Gurus** or **all Laghus**) applies strictly to the **1st syllable** (*prāsa-pūrvākṣaramu*), **never** to the 2nd syllable (*prāsākṣaramu*).  
* Provided the line maintains its overall metrical structure (*gaṇa-bhaṅgamu kāni vidhamugā*), the Prasa syllables themselves may freely mix heavy and light syllables across lines.

**Example 4 (*Kandamu* – Manu Charitra 1-67):**

* **Pre-Prasa Syllables:** **నా**, **దే**, **గా**, **గా** $\\rightarrow$ uniformly **Gurus**.  
* **Prasa Syllables:** **వు** (*laghu*), **వా** (*guru*), **విం** (*guru*), **వు** (*laghu*) $\\rightarrow$ freely mixed between Laghu and Guru.

**Example 5 (*Kandamu* – Mahabharata, Adi Parva 3-60):**

* **Pre-Prasa Syllables:** **సు**, **యు**, **న్నృ**, **రి** $\\rightarrow$ uniformly **Laghus**.  
* **Prasa Syllables:** **తు** (*laghu*), **తు** (*laghu*), **తి** (*laghu*), **తే** (*guru*) $\\rightarrow$ freely mixed between Laghu and Guru.

**Key Prosodic Distinctions Highlighted by the Full Corpus**

* **Sibilant Isolation in Samaprāsa:** The three sibilants—**శ** (*palatal*), **ష** (*retroflex*), and **స** (*dental*)—are verified individually as self-contained *Samaprāsa* series. (While acoustic *Prāsamaitri* may bridge **స** and **శ** under relaxation rules, pure *Samaprāsa* requires strict consonant identity).  
* **Liquid & Retroflex Disjunction:** **ర** (*Sādhu Rēpha*) is segregated from **ఱ** (*Śakaṭa Rēpha*), and **ల** (*dental lateral*) is strictly differentiated from **ళ** (*retroflex lateral*).  
* **Aspiration Independence:** Aspirated consonants (**ఖ, ఘ, ఠ, ధ, ఫ, భ**) are treated as completely distinct entities from their unaspirated counterparts, requiring exact aspirate-to-aspirate correspondence.

**5.5 Pūrṇabindu Prāsamu (Full-Nasal Prasa)**

* **Rule:** If the Prasa syllable in the first line is preceded by a full bindu (*pūrṇabindu* / *niṇḍusunna* / ం), the Prasa syllables across all subsequent lines must also be preceded by a full bindu. Conversely, if the first line lacks a preceding bindu, no other line may feature one. Compliance is strictly mandatory (*samaprāsamu*).  
* **Examples:**  
* *Appakavīyamu* (5-3-301): **పొందింప... / బృందావన... / బిందీవరాక్షి... / నందన...** (Prasa syllables: దిం, దా, దీ, ద; all preceded by 'ం' before 'ద').  
* *Bhāgavatam* (7-275): **ఇందుఁగలడందు... / సందేహము... / నెందెందు... / నందందే...** (Prasa syllables: ందు, ందే, ందె, ంద).

**5.5.3 Visarga Prāsamu (Visarga Prasa)**

* **Rule:** If the Prasa syllable in the first line is preceded by a visarga (ః), the Prasa syllables in all subsequent lines must likewise be preceded by a visarga. If the first line is not preceded by a visarga, no subsequent line may have one. The identical consonant must appear across all lines in this visarga-preceded state.  
* **Example:**  
* *Bhāgavatam* (10-Pūrvabhāga-1575): **దుఃఖింపన్... / చేతఃభేదంబెల్ల... / భూస్వఃఖేలజ్జనమెల్ల... / మేదఃఖండంబులు...** (Prasa syllables: ఃఖి, ఃఖే, ఃఖే, ఃఖం; all carry a visarga before 'ఖ').

**5.6 Saṁyuktākṣara Prāsamu (Conjunct Consonant Prasa)**

* **Rule:** If the Prasa syllable in the first line is a conjunct consonant (*saṁyuktākṣaramu*), the Prasa syllables in all subsequent lines must also be conjuncts. They must consist of the exact same constituent consonants combined in the exact same sequence (*kramamu*). If the first line is not a conjunct, subsequent lines cannot be conjuncts.  
* *Dvitvākṣara Prāsamu (5.6.2):* If the syllable is a geminate/doubled consonant, all lines must carry geminates of that identical consonant.  
* *R̥tva-Sahitamu (5.6.3):* If a conjunct contains the vocalic vowel *R̥*, the other lines need not contain *R̥*; they may combine with any vowel.  
* *Consonant Order (5.6.4):* Clusters with reversed consonant sequences (e.g., *rk* pairing with *kr*) violate the rule.  
* *Three-Consonant Clusters (5.6.5):* Clusters of three consonants must preserve all three consonants in identical order across all lines.  
* *Pūrṇabindupūrvaka Saṁyuktākṣara (5.6.7):* If the conjunct is preceded by a full bindu, all other lines must feature the identical conjunct preceded by a full bindu.  
* **Examples:**  
* *Bhāratam* (Ādi 1-6): **ఆశ్రిత... / పద్యాశ్రమ... / ర్ణాశ్రమ... / కాశ్రయుఁడు...** (Prasa syllables: శ్రి, శ్ర, శ్ర, శ్ర; all consist of *ś* \+ *r* \+ vowel).  
* *Sumati Śatakam* (Dvitva): **అక్కఱకు... / మ్రొక్కిన... / నెక్కిన... / గ్రక్కున...** (Prasa syllables: క్క, క్కి, క్కి, క్కు; all use geminate *k* \+ *k*).

**5.7 Ardhabindu Prāsamu (Uniform Half-Nasal Prasa)**

* **Rule:** When all Prasa syllables across every line of a stanza are preceded by a half-bindu (*ardhabindu* / *arasunna* / ఁ), it constitutes an *Ardhabindu Samaprāsamu*. Unlike the full bindu, maintaining an ardhabindu uniformly across all lines is optional and not compulsory.  
* **Examples:**  
* *Bhāratam* (Virāṭa 5-193): **వీఁక... / దాఁకిన... / రాఁకలి... / వ్రేఁకని...** (Prasa syllables: ఁక, ఁకి, ఁక, ఁక; all preceded by an ardhabindu).  
* *Dāśarathī Śatakam*: **నోఁచిన... / చోఁచేనివాఁడు... / నోరాఁచి... / దాఁచేనివాఁడు...** (Prasa syllables: ఁచి, ఁచే, ఁచి, ఁచే).

**5.8 Khaṇḍākhaṇḍa Prāsamu (Mixed Half-Nasal Prasa)**

* **Rule:** A relaxation rule where a Prasa syllable preceded by an ardhabindu (*khaṇḍabindu*) is permitted to rhyme with a syllable lacking an ardhabindu (*akhaṇḍa*). Appakavi limited this to unvoiced consonants (*paruṣas*: k, c, ṭ, t, p) following long vowels, while Ananta limited it strictly to 'p' and 'ṭ'; however, classical poetry demonstrates wider application across short/long vowels and voiced/unvoiced consonants.  
* **Examples:**  
* *Śrīkālahastīśvara Śatakam* (1-81): **ఇటువంటి... / పటుబుద్ధికి... / వటమాత్రంబఁట... / లఁట ముక్తి...** (Lines 1–3 use plain టు/ట, while Line 4 uses ఁట).  
* *Bhāratam* (Udyōga 4-3-255): **ఆ కొలువువారు... / లాకృతి... / బాఁకిడిన... / దాకారిత...** (Lines 1, 2, and 4 use plain కో/కృ/కా, while Line 3 uses ఁకి).

**5.9 Laghu 'Ya'-kāra Prāsamu (Light vs. Heavy 'Ya' Prasa)**

* **Rule:** An augment 'ya' inserted via sandhi (*yaḍāgamamu*) is categorized as light (*laghu*), whereas an inherent root 'ya' (*śabda-siddha*) is categorized as heavy (*alaghu*). In Prasa, light *ya* and heavy *ya* freely rhyme with each other.  
* **Examples:**  
* *Bhāratam* (Ādi 1-116): **ఈ యున్న... (augment/laghu) / బోయి... (root/alaghu) / త్యాయత... (root/alaghu) / మా యిన్ని... (augment/laghu)**.  
* *Bhāgavatam* (10-Pūrvabhāga-328): **ఓ యమ్మ... (augment/laghu) / మా యిండ్లను... (augment/laghu) / పోయెదమెక్కడికైనను... (root/alaghu) / మా యన్నల... (augment/laghu)**.

**5.10 Svavargaja Prāsamu ('Tha' and 'Dha' Class Rhyme)**

* **Rule:** Within the dental class (*ta-varga*), the second consonant (**థ** / *tha*) and the fourth consonant (**ధ** / *dha*) are permitted to rhyme with each other based on articulatory similarity. Both consonants must appear across the lines for the stanza to be classified under this rule. This compatibility also applies when preceded by a full bindu (**ంథ \- ంధ**) or when forming conjuncts (**ర్థ \- ర్ధ**).  
* **Examples:**  
* *Bhāgavatam* (6-7-138): **ఆ ధనేశు... (ధ) / ద్యాధరేంద్రులు... (ధ) / యాధికారులు... (ధి) / వీథి సర్వపథీనులు... (థి)** (Lines 1–3 use 'ధ', Line 4 uses 'థ').  
* *Bhāratam* (Āraṇya 4-159 \- Bindu form): **అంధక... (ంధ) / బాంధవ... (ంధ) / మనోగ్రంథి... (ంథి) / మోహాంధులు... (ంధు)** (Lines 1, 2, and 4 use 'ంధ', Line 3 uses 'ంథ').

**5.11 Ubhaya Prāsamu-1 ('Na' and 'Ṇa' Prasa)**

* **Rule:** In Sanskrit loanwords, retroflex **'ణ'** (*ṇa*) exists either as an inherent root sound (*sahaja*) or as an alternate derived from **'న'** (*na*) via phonetic sandhi rules (*ādeśa*). Appakavi permitted derived *ādeśa-ṇa* to rhyme with *sahaja-ṇa* (Rule 1\) and with dental *na* (Rule 2). Classical usage extends this principle so that both *ādeśa* and *sahaja* 'ṇa' freely rhyme with dental 'na'.  
* **Examples:**  
* *Bhāratam* (Udyōga 1-299): **ద్రోణుఁడు... (ādeśa-ṇa) / బ్రాణములు... (ādeśa-ṇa) / క్షీణ... (ādeśa-ṇa) / స్థాణునకైన... (sahaja-ṇa)**.  
* *Bhojarājīyam* (5-296): **ప్రాణాపాన... (ādeśa-ṇa) / దాన సమానములు... (na) / గానోపెడునది... (na) / ప్రాణంబననన్నమనఁగఁ... (ādeśa-ṇa)**.

**5.12 'Sa \- Śa' Prāsamu (Sibilant Compatibility Rhyme)**

* **Rule:** Dental **'స'** (*sa*) and palatal **'శ'** (*śa*) possess mutual rhyme compatibility (*prāsamaitri*) due to phonetic proximity. They are permitted to rhyme across lines, including in bindu environments (**ంస \- ంశ**), geminates (**స్స \- శ్శ**), and identical consonant conjuncts (**స్య \- శ్య**, **స్ర \- శ్ర**).  
* **Examples:**  
* *Vēmulavāḍa Bhīmakavi Chāṭu*: **బిసరుహ... (స) / మసకపుఁ... (స) / దశరథసూను... (శ) / పశుపతి... (శ)**.  
* *Bhāgavatam* (8-574): **ఆశాపాశము... (శ) / వారాశి... (శ) / గాసింబొందిరి... (స) / కామాశన్... (శ)**.

**5.13 Ubhaya Prāsamu-2 ('Sa \- Ṣa' Prasa)**

* **Rule:** Appakavi proposed that a substitute retroflex **'ష'** (*ādeśa-ṣa*, derived from dental *sa*) could rhyme with natural *ṣa* and dental *sa*. While matching *ādeśa-ṣa* with natural *ṣa* is accepted as standard identity rhyme (*Samaprāsamu*), pairing 'sa' with 'ṣa' lacks phonetic justification and is deprecated/not recommended for emulation.  
* **Examples:**  
* *Traiśaṅkavōpākhyānamu* (5-3-330): Lines 1–3 use *sahaja-ṣa* (**ద్విషునకు, వృషునకు, త్రిషునకు**), while Line 4 uses *ādeśa-ṣa* (**విషమాక్షునకు**).  
* *Sāmbavilāsamu* (5-3-331): Lines 1–3 use **'స'** (**వసుధా, రసదళ, విసరాంబుజ**), while Line 4 uses *ādeśa-ṣa* (**విషమ**).

**5.14 Abhēda Prāsamu ('La \- Ḷa' Identity Rhyme)**

* **Rule:** Dental **'ల'** (*la*) and retroflex **'ళ'** (*ḷa*) are treated as non-distinct (*abhēda*) and may freely rhyme across Sanskrit loanwords and native Telugu words. Although Appakavi also proposed 'la \- ḍa' and 'ḷa \- ḍa' rhymes based on interchangeable word doublets (*la-ḍayōr abhēdaḥ*), arbitrary pairings between 'la/ḷa' and 'ḍa' without doublets are unattested and deprecated.  
* **Examples:**  
* *Bhāratam* (Ādi 3-79 \- Sanskrit words): **అలఘుఁడు... (ల) / బలుఁడు... (ల) / ప్రళయాంతకుఁడు... (ళ) / లలిత... (ల)**.  
* *Haravilāsam* (4-139 \- Telugu words): **కులశైలంబులు... (ల) / గలఁగెఁ... (ల) / దలఁకిరి... (ల) / కళవళమయ్యెన్... (ళ)**.

**5.15 'R̥' Prāsamu (Vocalic 'R̥' rhyming with 'Ra')**

* **Rule:** A word-initial vocalic vowel **'ఋ'** (*R̥*) positioned in the Prasa slot is permitted to rhyme with the consonant **'ర'** (*repha*), as initial *R̥* is pronounced with a consonantal *r*\-onset and substitutes for *r* in sandhi. This also validates conjuncts combining with *R̥* (*hal-saṁyuta R̥-prāsamu*, e.g., **తృ** rhyming with **త్ర**).  
* **Examples:**  
* *Appakavīyamu* (5-3-359): **ధారుణి... (రు) / గా రేఫ... (రే) / నారికినై... (రి) / బా ఋభుపతి... (ఋ)**.  
* *Bhāratam* (Ādi 3-104): **ఆ ఋషికుమారు... (ఋ) / చీరలు... (ర) / హారము... (ర) / భారమునందొక్క... (ర)**.

**5.16 Saṁyuktāsaṁyukta Prāsamu (Conjunct vs. Simple Consonant Prasa)**

* **Rule:** A proposed relaxation allowing a consonant conjunct with *repha* (or *la*) to rhyme with the same simple consonant lacking *repha* (e.g., **క** rhyming with **క్ర**). Ananta restricted this solely to the numeral prefix *tri-* (*Triprāsamu*). Phonetically grounded occurrences are simply *hal-saṁyuta R̥-prāsamu* (e.g., **తృ** rhyming with **త్ర**); general *saṁyuktāsaṁyukta* lacks phonetic similarity and is deprecated.  
* **Examples:**  
* *Bhāratam* (Ādi 2-175 \- Hal-saṁyuta R̥-prāsa): **క్షత్రియ... (త్రి) / క్షత్రియ... (త్రి) / చరిత్రము... (త్ర) / మాంధాతృ... (తృ \= త్+ఋ)**.  
* *Bhāratam* (Āraṇya 6-103 \- Pure Saṁyuktāsaṁyukta): **ఆ క్రొక్కారను... (క్రొ) / కా... (కా) / కో... (కో) / కీ... (కీ)**.

**5.17 Rēphayuta Prāsamu (Repha-Conjunct Rules)**

* **Rule 1 (Krārakommu vs. Vaṭruvasuḍi):** A conjunct formed by a consonant \+ *r* \+ *u/ū* (*krārakommu*: **క్రు, క్రూ, గ్రు**) cannot rhyme with a consonant taking vocalic *R̥* (*vaṭruvasuḍi*: **కృ, కౄ**), despite acoustic similarities; *krārakommus* must strictly rhyme with other *repha* conjuncts.  
* **Rule 2 (Telugu Word-Initial Krāravaḍi):** When native Telugu words begin with a *repha* cluster (e.g., *krandu, krālu, krotta*), the initial cluster is pronounced lightly and does not prosodically lengthen the preceding word's final syllable into a *guru*. Therefore, all preceding syllables must independently be *gurus* on their own (inherently long vowels).  
* **Examples:**  
* *Appakavīyamu* (5-3-324 \- Krārakommu): **సంగ్రామ... (గ్రా) / చం గ్రుమ్మఱుచున్న... (గ్రు) / నం గ్రుచ్చి... (గ్రు) / చెం గ్రూరాస్త్రములు... (గ్రూ)**.  
* *Appakavīyamu* (5-3-325 \- Telugu Krāravaḍi): **ఈ క్రార... / లై క్రాలుచుఁ... / శ్రీ క్రొమ్ముడి... / వాక్రువ్వందరమె...** (Preceding syllables **ఈ, లై, శ్రీ, వా** are all independent *gurus*).

**5.18 Laghudvitva Prāsamu (Light-Conjunct Prasa)**

* **Rule:** In select native Telugu words, internal medial *repha* clusters (e.g., in *adrucu, edruci, padruci, cidrupallu*) are pronounced lightly and do not make the preceding short vowel heavy (*guru*). If such a light conjunct occupies the Prasa slot, all lines must consistently employ light conjuncts so that all preceding syllables remain *laghus*.  
* **Examples:**  
* *Bhāratam* (Udyōga 4-109): **ఎద్రిచిన... (ద్రి) / పద్రిచిన... (ద్రి) / విద్రుపఁగఁ... (ద్రు) / జిద్రుపలు... (ద్రు)** (Preceding syllables **ఎ, ప, వి, చి** all remain *laghus*).  
* *Rāmāyaṇa Kalpavṛkṣamu* (Bāla, Dhanus 91): **చిద్రుపలు... (ద్రు) / యద్రువగ... (ద్రు) / నెద్రిచిన... (ద్రి) / విద్రుపలుగాఁ... (ద్రు)**.

**5.19 Vikalpa Prāsamu (Alternative-Form Sandhi Prasa)**

* **Rule:** In Sanskrit nasal sandhi (*Anunāsika Sandhi*), a stop followed by a nasal optionally produces two valid forms: one with the class nasal (5th consonant) and one with the class stop (3rd consonant). Under *Vikalpa Prāsamu*, a poet may write the more common 5th-consonant form while pairing it against lines that use the 3rd-consonant cluster (e.g., writing **వాజ్నియమ** and rhyming it with **అగ్ని, భగ్న**, based on the alternative doublet **వాగ్నియమ**). This applies only when sandhi occurs between independent words, not obligatory grammatical suffixes like *\-maya* or *\-mātra*.  
* **Examples:**  
* *Bhāratam* (Ādi 7-153): **అగ్ని... (గ్ని) / భుజ్నివహము... (జ్ని, scanning as భుగ్ని) / డగ్నుల... (గ్ను) / యగ్నియనన్... (గ్ని)**.  
* *Lakṣaṇa Śirōmaṇi* (4-413): **పద్మా... (ద్మా) / పద్మావతిఁ... (ద్మా) / ద్యన్మోదంబునఁ... (న్మో, scanning as ద్యద్మో) / పద్మము... (ద్మ)**.

**5.20 Anunāsika Prāsamu (Nasal-Geminates vs. Bindu-Nasal Prasa)**

* **Rule:** Defined as two sub-rules: (1) geminate 'n' (**న్న**) rhyming with full-bindu 'n' (**ంన**), and (2) geminate 'm' (**మ్మ**) rhyming with full-bindu 'm' (**ంమ**). These arose to account for doublets like *sannidhi / saṁnidhi* and *sammada / saṁmada*, or sandhi where an inflected *drutamu* surfaces as a bindu before 'm'. These forms are non-standard in orthography and deprecated.  
* **Examples:**  
* *Appakavīyamu* (5-3-341): **మిన్నేఱు... (న్నే) / తన్నాభిని... (న్నా) / సాంనిధ్యము... (ంని) / సంనుతి... (ంను)**.  
* *Appakavīyamu* (5-3-342): **తమ్ములను... (మ్ము) / మమ్ముల... (మ్ము) / భక్తిమ్మురసంహరు... (మ్ము) / సంమదమున... (ంమ)**.

**5.21 Prāsamaitri Prāsamu ('Mba' and 'Mma' Prasa)**

* **Rule:** A proposed rule allowing a full-bindu 'ba' (**ంబ**) to rhyme with a geminate 'ma' (**మ్మ**) based on noun inflection doublets ending in *\-mmu* / *\-mbu* (e.g., *bimbamu $\\rightarrow$ bimbambu / bimbammu*). Because these sounds possess distinct articulatory differences, early classical poets avoided them, and the rule is deprecated.  
* **Examples:**  
* *Sukavimanōrañjanamu* citation of *Bhāratam* (Ādi 1-76): **ఇమ్ముగ... (మ్ము) / బమ్మున... (మ్ము) / ధ్రమ్మను... (మ్మ) / ద్యుంబరముం... (ంబ)**.  
* *Jaimini Bhāratamu* (7-145): **అమ్మఖవాజి... (మ్మ) / మోవం బసివెట్టి... (ంబ) / గ్రంబున... (ంబు) / త్రంబయి... (ంబ)**.

**5.22 'Ra \- Ṟa' Prāsa / Prāsavairamu (Exclusion of Sādhu and Śakaṭa Repha)**

* **Rule:** Soft *repha* (**ర** / *Sādhu Rēpha*) and hard *repha* (**ఱ** / *Śakaṭa Rēpha*) are strictly forbidden from rhyming with one another. Standard **ర** may only rhyme with **ర**, and **ఱ** may only rhyme with **ఱ**. Mixing them in the Prasa slot is designated as *Prāsavairamu* (Rhyme Hostility/Conflict).  
* **Examples:**  
* *Sādhu Rēpha* only (*Bhāratam*, Ādi 1-26): **సారమతిన్... (ర) / లోనారసి... (ర) / నారుచిరార్థ... (రు) / మహాభారత... (ర)**.  
* *Śakaṭa Rēpha* only (*Bhāgavatam* 8-130): **ఎఱుఁగుదు... (ఱు) / మఱవను... (ఱ) / మఱతునని... (ఱ) / మఱవక... (ఱ)**.

### 5.23 సంధిగత ప్రాసము (Sandhigata Prāsamu – Sandhi-Derived Prasa)

* **Core Principle:** When a Prasa syllable is created through *sandhi* (morphophonemic compounding), the rhyme is evaluated strictly on the resulting phonetic consonant, not its pre-sandhi underlying root. Syllables existing naturally in a base word (*śabda-siddhamu*) and syllables formed dynamically through sandhi (*sandhigatamu*) freely rhyme together.  
* **5.23.1 Svara Sandhi (Vowel Sandhi):**  
* Because vowels do not govern Prasa, vowel mergers (*akāra, ikāra, ukāra sandhis*) that alter only the vowel of the second syllable do not disturb the consonant rhyme.  
* *Example:* **తాను \+ ఊరక \= తానూరక** (syllable **నూ**) rhyming with inherent root **'న'** words like **అనలు** or **దానవ**.  
* **5.23.2 Druta Sandhi / Saralādēśa (ద్రుత సంధి):**  
* When an inflected *drutamu* (**న్**) precedes an unvoiced stop (*paruṣamu*), the stop softens to a voiced stop (*saralamu*) and the *drutamu* surfaces as a full bindu (e.g., **పుత్రున్ \+ కని \= పుత్రుంగని**).  
* The resulting syllable is treated as a *bindupūrvaka ‘ga’-kāramu* (**ంగ**) and must be matched across lines under *Bindupūrvaka Prāsamu*.  
* **5.23.4 Gasadadavādēśa Prāsamu (గసడదవాదేశ ప్రాసము):**  
* When initial *paruṣas* (**క, చ, ట, త, ప**) optionally undergo mutation into **గ, స, డ, ద, వ** in compound structures, the resulting mutated consonant serves as the Prasa consonant.  
* A light/substitute **'వ'** derived from **'ప'** (e.g., **వాఁడు \+ కొట్టె \= వాఁడుగొట్టె**) freely rhymes with root **'వ'** (*śabda-siddha va-kāramu*).

### 5.24 ఇతర ప్రాసలు (Itara Prāsalu – Marginal & Incompatible Consonant Pairings)

This section systematically reviews disputed consonant pairs that poets occasionally attempted or debated, evaluating whether they possess genuine *Prāsamaitri* (acoustic compatibility) or constitute metrical defects (*Asamaprāsas*):

* **5.24.1 ద \- ధ ప్రాస (Da \- Dha Prāsa):**  
* **Rule:** Pairing unaspirated dental **'ద'** (*da*) with aspirated dental **'ధ'** (*dha*).  
* **Status:** Deprecated (*apraśastamu*). While **థ \- ధ** share phonetic affinity as aspirates within the dental class (Section 5.10), pairing an unaspirated stop (**ద**) with an aspirate (**ధ**) lacks articulatory balance.  
* *Example:* Attempting to rhyme **ముదము** (*mudamu*) with **బుధులు** (*budhulu*).  
* **5.24.2 ట \- డ ప్రాస (Ṭa \- Ḍa Prāsa):**  
* **Rule:** Pairing unvoiced retroflex **'ట'** (*ṭa*) with voiced retroflex **'డ'** (*ḍa*).  
* **Status:** Impermissible (*chelladu*). The unvoiced/voiced acoustic disparity in retroflex stops causes an audible break in the rhyme cadence (*prāsa-bhaṅgamu*).  
* *Example:* Attempting to rhyme **మాట** (*māṭa*) with **జాడ** (*jāḍa*).  
* **5.24.3 ద \- డ ప్రాస (Da \- Ḍa Prāsa):**  
* **Rule:** Pairing dental **'ద'** (*da*) with retroflex **'డ'** (*ḍa*).  
* **Status:** Defective and invalid. Dental and retroflex articulatory zones are strictly distinct. Occurrences in manuscripts represent regional dialect corruptions or copyist errors.  
* *Example:* Attempting to rhyme **పదము** (*padamu*) with **కడుపు** (*kaḍupu*).  
* **5.24.4 డ \- ఢ ప్రాస (Ḍa \- Ḍha Prāsa):**  
* **Rule:** Pairing unaspirated retroflex **'డ'** (*ḍa*) with aspirated retroflex **'ఢ'** (*ḍha*).  
* **Status:** Universally rejected across all meters. The stark phonetic difference between unaspirated and aspirated retroflex stops admits no *Prāsamaitri*.  
* *Example:* Attempting to rhyme **జడ** (*jaḍa*) with **మూఢ** (*mūḍha*).  
* **5.24.5 స్వవర్గజప్రాస \- విస్తరణ (Svavargaja Prāsa – Class Generalization Test):**  
* **Rule:** Analyzes whether the license granted to dental **థ \- ధ** can be generalized to the 2nd and 4th consonants of other vargas (**క-ఖ, గ-ఘ, చ-ఛ, ట-ఠ, ప-ఫ, బ-భ**).  
* **Verdict:** Non-extendable. Classical authorities restrict *Svavargaja Prāsa* exclusively to **థ \- ధ**; attempting class-neighbor rhymes in guttural, retroflex, or labial series is non-standard.  
* **5.24.6 ర \- ల ప్రాస (Ra \- La Prāsa):**  
* **Rule:** Pairing liquid **'ర'** (*ra*) with lateral **'ల'** (*la*).  
* **Status:** Strictly forbidden in Telugu verse (*niṣiddhamu*). Although Sanskrit grammar recognizes occasional interchangeability (*ralayōr abhēdaḥ*) for semantic punning (*śleṣa*), Telugu prosody enforces an absolute barrier between *repha* and *la-kāra*.  
* *Example:* Attempting to rhyme **తరము** (*taramu*) with **తలము** (*talamu*).

### 5.25 శాంతప్రాసము (Śānta Prāsamu – Pacified Prasa)

* **Definition:** A non-standard, irregular rhyme (*Asamaprāsa*) where a consonant's phonetic sharpness is softened or muted due to phonetic sandhi or trailing nasal resonances.  
* **Status:** Non-canonical and deprecated (*anusaraṇīyamu kādu*). Classical treatises document it historically to explain rare, irregular lines in early texts, but advise poets against utilizing it.

---

### 5.26 పూర్ణార్ధబిందు ప్రాసము (Pūrṇārdhabindu Prāsamu – Mixed Full and Half Nasal)

* **Definition:** Rhyming a Prasa syllable preceded by a full bindu (*pūrṇabindu* / నిండుసున్న / ం) against a syllable preceded by a half-bindu (*ardhabindu* / అరసున్న / ఁ).  
* **Phonetic Defect:**  
* A full bindu (**ం**) carries true consonant value, adding weight to the preceding vowel and rendering it prosodically heavy (**Guru**).  
* A half-bindu (**ఁ**) represents a historical nasal vowel trace with zero phonetic weight; it leaves the preceding short vowel light (**Laghu**).  
* **Verdict:** Defective (*prāsa-doṣamu*). Pairing **ం** with **ఁ** directly breaches the **Prāsa Pūrvākṣara Niyamamu** (prosodic weight uniformity of the line's first syllable) and is categorized as an unapproved *Asamaprāsa*.  
* *Example:* Pairing a line starting with **కొండ...** (*koṇḍa* – Line 1 syllable is Guru) with **గోఁడు...** (*gōఁḍu* – where the half-bindu cannot equate to a full nasal).

---

### 5.28 అధికప్రాసము (Adhika Prāsamu – Redundant / Surplus Prasa)

* **Definition:** An irregular rhyme where extra consonantal elements or redundant syllables are forced into the rhyming position.  
* **Forms:**  
1. An asymmetrical extra consonant in a conjunct cluster that appears in some lines but is missing in others.  
2. Extending the rhyming requirement beyond the mandatory 2nd syllable into the 3rd syllable (*dvyakṣara prāsa*) irregularly across lines without structural symmetry.  
* **Status:** Classified under *Asamaprāsas* that introduce metric clutter; deprecated in formal metered verse.
